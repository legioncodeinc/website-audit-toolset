## What is a social media audit?

A social media audit is a structured review of all your social media accounts that compiles and analyzes your performance across platforms.

Think of your social media audit as a report card: It identifies strengths, areas for improvement, and next steps. It’s typically run by social media managers, marketing directors, or agency teams, and it covers both your organic and paid presence. It also helps marketing leaders evaluate social ROI, update content strategy, and make smarter budget decisions.

## Why run a social media audit?

A social media audit helps you make better strategic decisions and gives marketing leaders the evidence they need to justify where budget and effort should go, especially when 59% of CMOs report insufficient budget to execute their strategy. Here’s what a good audit surfaces:

- Underperforming channels: See which platforms deserve more (or less) investment.
- Content performance: Learn which formats drive the most engagement or conversions.
- Proof of ROI: Give leadership clear numbers that connect social activity to business results, particularly with global social ad spend reaching $276 billion in 2025.
- Team alignment: Tie day-to-day marketing work back to company goals.
- Branding issues: Catch inconsistencies across profiles before they hurt trust.
- Competitive opportunities: Spot gaps and openings your competitors are missing.

In short, an audit turns scattered data into a clear picture of what to do next.

## Social media audit vs. social media strategy

A social media audit is a diagnostic snapshot of where you are, while a social media strategy is the forward-looking plan for where you’re going. The two work together: the audit tells you what’s happening now, and the strategy uses those findings to map your next moves.

An audit reviews your accounts, performance, branding, and audience at a single point in time. It answers “what’s working and what isn’t?” A social media strategy is the ongoing playbook that sets goals, defines your content approach, and guides how you show up over the coming months. Put simply: The audit tells you where you are, and the strategy tells you where you’re going. Run the audit first, then build or refine your strategy on top of it.

## How to perform a social media audit in 7 steps

To conduct a social media audit, follow these steps:

1. List all your social media accounts
2. Review branding and profile consistency
3. Identify your top-performing content
4. Evaluate each channel’s performance
5. Benchmark against competitors
6. Understand your audience on each platform
7. Review compliance and governance

### 1. List all your social media accounts

Jot down all your social media accounts to start, from the platforms you use daily to ones you may have neglected. Active or inactive, it’s important to catalog your online presence as part of your social media audit. Don’t forget newer platforms like Threads and Bluesky, which many brands added to the mix over the past year.

In addition, think about the accounts you don’t have yet. With 5.75 billion social media users in 2026, the landscape keeps expanding. Are there platforms you haven’t considered?

In larger organizations, this step also means checking for rogue or unauthorized accounts created by different departments or regions. Tracking these down keeps your presence consistent and protects your brand.

You don’t need to be active on every network, but an audit is a good opportunity to add new ideas to your future social media strategy.

#### Where to find this info:

Search each major social network for your brand name (and even your products). Not only might this uncover long-forgotten Facebook groups, but it’s also a chance to catch any imposter or misleading accounts that you can then shut down or report.

Pro tip 💡: Set up a social media monitoring program to catch any future fake accounts.

### 2. Review branding and profile consistency

Check each of your social media accounts to see whether they align with your current brand style guidelines.

Review the following areas for each social account:

- Profile and cover images. Do your images reflect your current branding and adhere to each social network’s image size requirements?
- Profile/bio text. Is every field in your social media profile filled in accurately? Does the copy match your tone and voice guidelines?
- Username. Try to use the same username across all social channels, though having more than one account per network is okay if they serve different purposes. (For example, we have X accounts like@Hootsuite and@Hootsuite_Help.)
- Contact information and links. Does the URL in your profile go to the correct website or landing page? Are your phone number and contact email still correct?
- Pinned posts (if applicable). Evaluate your pinned posts to ensure they’re still appropriate and up-to-date.
- Verification. Is your account verified with a blue checkmark badge? If not, should you try? We have guides on how to get verified on Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, and X (formerly Twitter) if you want to pursue this.

#### Where to find this info:

Visit each of your social profiles and see how you’re showing up online. Be sure to click on any links to see if they need to be replaced.
