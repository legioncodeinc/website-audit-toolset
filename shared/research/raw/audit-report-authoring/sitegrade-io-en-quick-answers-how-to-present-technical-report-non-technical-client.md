# Present Technical Reports to Non-Technical Clients
SiteGrade Team March 26, 2026 3 min read
## Lead with business impact, not technical jargon
Your client does not care that the X-Frame-Options header is missing. They care that competitors could embed their site in a fake page and steal customer data. Every technical finding needs a business translation: what does this cost them in money, reputation, or lost customers? If you cannot explain it in those terms, the client will not act on it.
The first page of your report should answer three questions: what is broken, how bad is it, and how much will it cost to fix. Everything else goes in the appendix.
## Structure that works
1. **Executive summary (1 page)** - Overall site health score, top three priorities, estimated cost of inaction. This is the only page most decision-makers will read.
2. **Risk overview (1-2 pages)** - Color-coded findings: red for critical, yellow for needs attention, green for passing. Group by business area (security, speed, legal compliance), not by technical category.
3. **Recommendations (1 page)** - Prioritized action items with estimated cost and timeline. Quick wins first, then medium-term, then long-term projects.
4. **Technical appendix** - Full details for the development team. Headers, configurations, code snippets. The client will never read this, but their IT person will.

## Analogies that land
Missing security headers? "Your front door is unlocked." No SSL certificate? "You are sending customer mail on postcards instead of sealed envelopes." Slow loading times? "Your shop has a revolving door that takes 8 seconds to open." These analogies are not dumbing down - they are translating expertise into the language of business decisions.
## FAQ
### How detailed should the report be?
The executive summary should fit on one page. The full report including technical appendix can be 10-20 pages, but structure it so the client only needs to read the first three. Decision-makers want the big picture. Their dev team wants the details. Serve both in one document.
### Should I include pricing for recommendations?
Yes. Findings without pricing are just a wish list. Give at least a range for each recommendation so the client can budget. Separate quick wins (under 500 EUR) from major projects (5,000+ EUR). This turns your report into a proposal and makes it easy to say yes.
### What format works best for presenting?
A live walkthrough beats a PDF sent by email. Share your screen, go through the executive summary, show two or three critical findings with visual evidence, and end with the action plan. Twenty minutes is enough. Leave the full report as a reference document they can share internally.
