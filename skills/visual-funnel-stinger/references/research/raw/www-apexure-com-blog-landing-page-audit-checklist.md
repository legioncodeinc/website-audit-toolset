# The Landing Page Audit Checklist We Use After 3,000+ Projects
URL: https://www.apexure.com/blog/landing-page-audit-checklist
Published: 2026-03-29

Apexure has audited landing pages across CRO engagements for over 10 years, across 3,000+ projects and 300+ clients, from startups to Fortune 500 companies like Zillow and Rocket Companies.

## Key Takeaways

- Message mismatch is the #1 conversion killer: when the ad promises one thing and the page delivers another, bounce rates spike. This single issue shows up in over half of all audits the source runs.
- Audit 9 pillars, not 100 surface items: above-the-fold, copy, CTAs, forms, trust, design, speed, mobile, and SEO. Depth beats breadth.
- Forms are where intent dies; every unnecessary field is a reason to leave. B2C: max 5 fields. B2B: max 7 unless using multi-step.
- Speed is money: a one-second delay in load time reduces conversions by approximately 7%. Check both lab and field data.
- Audit quarterly, check monthly: full 9-pillar audit every quarter, focused check on conversion rates and speed monthly. If metrics shift 15%+, trigger a full audit immediately.
- Use the EPIC framework to prioritise fixes: score each finding by Experiment potential, Priority, Impact, and Cost before implementing.

## Why most landing page audits fail

The typical landing page audit checks surface-level items: is the logo there, does the button work, is the phone number correct. These are table stakes, not audit items. A real audit asks harder questions: Is the headline answering the right search intent? Is the form asking for information that kills mobile completion rates? Is the hero image adding 2.3 seconds to Largest Contentful Paint?

The first check on any new client's pages is the gap between what the ad promises and what the page delivers. Message mismatch is called the single biggest conversion killer, and it rarely appears on generic checklists. One cited client example: $30,000/month on Google Ads driving traffic to a page converting at 1.02%; after audit and redesign, the same page hit 6.09%, a 500% increase confirmed through A/B testing.

## Part 1: Above-the-Fold audit

The first screen a visitor sees carries disproportionate weight. Above-the-fold issues account for roughly 40% of all audit findings, per the source's own project history.

| Checkpoint | What to Check | Common Failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline clarity | Does the headline state the value proposition in under 10 words? | Clever puns that require context to understand |
| Ad-to-page match | Does the headline mirror the ad copy or search query that brought the visitor here? | Generic brand headline with no keyword alignment |
| Hero image relevance | Does the visual reinforce the offer, or is it decorative stock? | Generic handshake photo that could be on any business page |
| CTA visibility | Is the primary CTA visible without scrolling on all devices? | CTA pushed below the fold by oversized hero images |
| Load time (LCP) | Does the largest above-fold element render within 2.5 seconds? | Uncompressed hero image at 3MB+ causing 5s+ LCP |
| Trust signal | Is at least one trust element (logo bar, review badge, stat) visible above the fold? | All social proof buried below the third scroll |

Example cited: a B2C agency's landing page where the Google Ads headline promised "Free Consultation" and the page headline said "Change Your Business." Visitors bounced at 78%. After aligning the messaging, conversion rate jumped from 1.02% to 6.09%.

The hero has one job: confirm the visitor is in the right place within three seconds. Everything that doesn't serve that single purpose (secondary CTAs, feature lists, animations competing for attention) should be stripped from the hero.

## Part 2: copy and messaging audit

- Benefit vs. Feature ratio: count benefits and features separately. If features outnumber benefits by more than 2:1, the copy needs rewriting.
- Scan-ability test: can a visitor understand the full offer by reading only the headline, subheads, and CTA button text?
- Specificity check: replace vague claims ("our solution helps businesses grow") with measurable ones ("helped 300+ clients increase conversions by an average of 80%").
- Objection handling: does the copy address the top 2-3 objections for this audience (e.g., enterprise buyers need procurement process answers; startup founders need cost clarity)?
- Reading level: landing page copy should hit Grade 6-8 reading level.

Cited example: a landing page built for Board Agenda, targeting UK board directors and senior corporate executives, with copy and a multi-step form matched specifically to that persona, achieved 82 conversions out of 294 visitors, a 27.89% conversion rate.

## Part 3: CTA audit

- Button contrast ratio: minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio against its background, tested with a contrast checker rather than by eye.
- Action-specific copy: "Submit" converts 3% less than "Get My Free Quote" on average, per the source. Button text should complete the sentence "I want to ___."
- Single primary action: count competing CTAs above the fold; more than one possible action (Watch Video, Sign Up, Chat Now) means the page lacks focus.
- Placement frequency: CTAs checked after each major section; the source's own Hotjar scroll-map data across 60 blog posts is cited but the archived fetch cuts off mid-sentence before stating the specific finding.
